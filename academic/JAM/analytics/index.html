04 Question Type & Marking Scheme Dynamics

In the CBT era, the question paper is segregated into three distinct formats with radically different risk-reward profiles:

MCQ Multiple Choice Questions
Total: 525 Questions (59.3%)
• Sec A carries 30 Qs (10 at 1M + 20 at 2M = 50 Marks).
• Negative Marking: $-1/3$ for 1-mark and $-2/3$ for 2-mark questions.
• Highest risk of score erosion: requires elimination of distractors.
MSQ Multiple Select Questions
Total: 120 Questions (13.6%)
• Sec B carries 10 Qs at 2 Marks each (20 Marks).
• Zero Negative Marking: No penalty for incorrect attempts.
• Strict All-or-Nothing Rule: Full credit awarded only if all correct choices are ticked; no partial credit.
NAT Numerical Answer Type
Total: 240 Questions (27.1%)
• Sec C carries 20 Qs (10 at 1M + 10 at 2M = 30 Marks).
• Zero Negative Marking: Solved via mouse on a virtual keypad.
• Official Tolerance Bounds: Official keys include continuous numerical tolerance intervals (e.g. 1.41 to 1.43).
🎯 Tactical Scoring Insight: The 50-Mark Zero-Penalty Buffer
Sections B (MSQ) and C (NAT) together comprise 30 questions worth exactly 50% of the entire paper (50 marks) with strictly ZERO negative marking. Candidates who cultivate computational precision in NATs and rigorous theorem checking in MSQs can aggressively attempt all 50 marks without any penalty risk, establishing a tremendous competitive edge.

06 Classical Out of Syllabus Topics (87 Questions)

Between 2005 and 2021, the official syllabus underwent several periodic revisions by the organizing IITs. In total, 87 questions (9.8% of the repository) pertain to topics that were formally removed from the contemporary syllabus:

⚠️ Breakdown of Discontinued Topics
• Vector Calculus: Green’s theorem, Stokes’ theorem, Gauss Divergence theorem, line and surface integrals, conservative vector fields.
• Partial Differential Equations (PDEs): First-order quasilinear PDEs (Lagrange's method), second-order wave & heat equations.
• Point-Set Topology: Open/closed sets in $\mathbb{R}^n$, compactness, connectedness outside standard metric analysis.
• Linear Programming (LPP): Simplex method, graphical optimization, duality.
📚 Era Distribution of OOS Questions
• Classic Era (2005–2014): 37 Questions (high weightage in early exams).
• Established CBT Era (2015–2021): 50 Questions (predominantly Vector Calculus and elementary PDEs before removal).
• Recent CBT Era (2022–2026): 0 Questions (the current syllabus has remained strictly stable without these topics).
⚠️ Pedagogical Recommendation on Out of Syllabus PYQs
In the main portal's PYQ Tracker, these 87 questions can be excluded with a single click ("Mark Out of Syllabus as Unnecessary") so candidates can focus 100% on the active 798 in-syllabus questions. 09 Strategic Preparation Takeaways for Aspirants

Based on quantitative analysis of 885 official questions over 22 years, here are five empirical principles for maximizing your IIT JAM Mathematics score:

1. Anchor Your Preparation in Real Analysis
Real Analysis constitutes 32.3% of all questions (621 marks). A candidate who scores above 85% in Sequences, Series, and Single Variable Calculus is statistically virtually guaranteed a top-200 All India Rank.
2. Capitalize on the 50-Mark Zero-Negative Penalty Buffer
MSQ (Section B) and NAT (Section C) questions carry zero negative penalty. Never leave an MSQ or NAT blank! In NATs, always calculate to at least 2 decimal places to comfortably land within official tolerance intervals.
3. Treat ODE and Group Theory as Guaranteed Points
Ordinary Differential Equations (113 Qs) and Groups (101 Qs) have the most recurring canonical problem structures (exact ODEs, Wronskians, Cauchy-Euler, cyclic group generators, permutation parities, Sylow applications). These are the fastest marks on the paper.
4. Adopt Tiered Difficulty Drills
Use the platform's difficulty filter to progress methodically: Easy/Medium (345 Qs) $\to$ build fundamental computational speed $\to$ then tackle Hard/Hardest (480 Qs) to refine edge-case reasoning and multi-statement MSQ analysis.
5. Simulate Under Strict Exam Conditions
Knowledge without time management results in unattempted questions. Practice with the online CBT simulator's 180-minute countdown timer and virtual calculator to build psychological stamina before test day.
