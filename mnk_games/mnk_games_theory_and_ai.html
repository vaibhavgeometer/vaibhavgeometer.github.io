1. Introduction to (m,n,k)-Games

An (m, n, k)-game is an abstract combinatorial board game played between two players on an m × n rectangular grid. The two players take turns placing their respective marks (X and O) on any unoccupied square. The objective is to be the first to align k marks consecutively along a straight line — horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.

If all m × n squares are filled without either player achieving k in a row, the game ends in a theoretical draw.

Game Configuration: (m, n, k)
• m ≥ 1 : Number of rows
• n ≥ 1 : Number of columns
• k ≥ 1 : Contiguous streak required to win (with k ≤ max(m, n))
• Total Board State Space: 3^(m × n) possible configurations

These games form a cornerstone of Maker-Maker positional games in combinatorial game theory. While standard 3×3 Tic-Tac-Toe ((3, 3, 3)) has a modest search tree of 255,168 games and 5,478 legal positions, scaling the board to 15×15 Gomoku ((15, 15, 5)) or our maximum 32×32 engine ((32, 32, k)) yields state spaces of over 10^488 positions — dwarfing the complexity of Chess (~10^43) and Go (~10^170).

2. Fundamental Game-Theoretic Theorems

Because (m, n, k)-games are finite, two-player, zero-sum, perfect information games without chance, Zermelo's 1913 Theorem establishes that every position has an exact, determinate game-theoretic value: either First-Player Win, Draw, or Second-Player Win under optimal play.

⚖️ Theorem: John Nash's Strategy-Stealing Argument (1952)

Statement: In any (m, n, k)-game, the second player (O) can never have a winning strategy.

Proof by Contradiction: Suppose player 2 possessed a guaranteed winning strategy S. Player 1 places their first stone on an arbitrary cell c_1. Player 1 then mentally treats this stone as a phantom neutral stone and adopts strategy S as if Player 1 were the second player. If strategy S ever instructs Player 1 to play on c_1 (which is already occupied), Player 1 simply chooses any remaining unoccupied square instead. Because having an extra stone on the board can never disadvantage a player in an (m,n,k) game (placing a stone is purely monotonic and non-destructive), Player 1's imitation of S will win at least as quickly as S would. This contradicts the assumption that S was a winning strategy exclusively for player 2. ▪

Consequently, every (m, n, k)-game is either a First-Player Win (X) or a Theoretical Draw. A second-player win is mathematically impossible under optimal play!

Pairing Strategies & Ramsey Theory

How does player 2 force a draw? The primary constructive method in combinatorial game theory is the Pairing Strategy (pioneered by Hales & Jewett in 1963 and Erdős & Selfridge in 1973). If Player 2 can partition all cells of the board into disjoint pairs such that every winning line contains at least one whole pair, Player 2 simply responds to every move of Player 1 by playing the paired square.

Since Player 1 can never claim both squares in any pair, Player 1 can never complete a full line of length k.

4. Complete Tablebases, Partial Tablebases & Opening Books in StockTic

In computational game engines (such as chess and endgame solvers), players frequently wonder how precalculated knowledge is maintained: for which specific values of (m,n,k) is a complete endgame tablebase saved?, for which configurations is a partial tablebase available?, and how are opening books stored?

Because generalized (m,n,k)-games span thousands of rule variations on board sizes ranging from 1×1 to 32×32 with k ∈ [1, 8], downloading gigabytes of static binary tablebase or opening book files is prohibitive for a zero-latency web application hosted on GitHub Pages. Instead, StockTic utilizes an innovative multi-tiered hybrid architecture:

A. Complete Endgame Tablebases

A complete endgame tablebase contains the exact, uncompromised game-theoretic value (Win, Loss, or Draw) and optimal move for every reachable legal game state without heuristic guesswork or depth truncation.

In this project, complete endgame tablebases are saved and computed for the following exact parameter sets:

  • All Boards with m × n ≤ 9 for any k ≥ 1: StockTic includes an exact retrograde Minimax engine (exactMinimax). For any board containing 9 or fewer cells, the engine exhaustively solves the complete game tree down to all terminal leaves and caches every visited board key in the in-memory Transposition Table/Tablebase map (ttMap).
    Specifically, this provides exact, mathematically perfect tablebases for:
    • Classic 3×3 Tic-Tac-Toe ((3, 3, 3)): All 5,478 reachable board configurations (765 symmetry-distinct classes, 549 terminal positions) are completely traversed, proving the theoretical draw (0.00) and returning optimal moves with zero heuristic error.
    • Small 3×3 Variants: (3, 3, 2) (First-Player Win in 3 ply) and (3, 3, 1) (Trivial Win in 1 ply).
    • Rectangular grids ≤ 9 cells: (2, 2, k), (2, 3, k), (3, 2, k), (2, 4, k), and (4, 2, k) for all k ≤ 4.
    • Linear strip grids ≤ 9 cells: (1, 1, k) through (1, 9, k) (and (9, 1, k)) for all k.
  • Trivial Boundary Solvers:
    • k = 1 (Any m, n from 1×1 to 32×32): Complete 1-ply tablebase — the first player wins instantaneously on move 1 on any square of the grid (+100).
    • max(m, n) < k (Any m, n): Complete 0-ply tablebase — forming k in a row is geometrically impossible, proving an immutable theoretical draw with 0 winning paths.

B. Partial Tablebases & Analytical Proof Oracles

For board sizes greater than 9 cells (mn > 9), storing an exhaustive game tree on disk is computationally impossible (e.g. 4×4 has 3^16 ≈ 4.3 × 10^7 states, while 15×15 Gomoku has 10^105 states). Instead, StockTic provides four layers of partial tablebase knowledge:

  1. Analytical Proof Oracle (getTheoreticalValue(m, n, k)): Hardcoded directly into the engine is an exact closed-form lookup function embodying over six decades of published combinatorial proofs:
    • k = 2: (1, 2, 2) and (2, 1, 2) are theoretical draws; all other boards with max(m, n) ≥ 2 are forced First-Player Wins.
    • k = 3: Strips with min(m, n) < 3 are theoretical draws; (3, 3, 3) is a solved draw; boards with min(m, n) ≥ 3 and max(m, n) ≥ 4 (such as (4, 3, 3) and (4, 4, 3)) are forced First-Player Wins.
    • k = 4: All boards with max(m, n) ≤ 5 (including (4, 4, 4) and (5, 5, 4)) are proven theoretical draws (Patashnik, 1980). On (m, 4, 4), games are draws for m ≤ 8 and forced wins for m ≥ 9. Any grid with min(m, n) ≥ 5 and max(m, n) ≥ 6 (such as (6, 5, 4)) is a forced First-Player Win.
    • k = 5: All grids with m, n ≤ 8 (including (5, 5, 5), (6, 6, 5), (7, 7, 5), (8, 8, 5)) are proven theoretical draws (Allis 1993, Hsu & Ko 2018). Large open boards with m, n ≥ 15 (such as (15, 15, 5) Gomoku and (19, 19, 5)) are proven First-Player Wins (Victor Allis 1993).
    • k ≥ 8: All boards of arbitrary size are proven theoretical draws by pairing strategies (Plambeck 1985 / Zebedin 1995).
    • k = 6, 7: If max(m, n) ≤ k, Erdős-Selfridge pairing forces a draw; if max(m, n) > k, the theoretical value is an open problem (conjectured draw).
    • m = n = k with k ≥ 4: Always a theoretical draw.
  2. Ply-0 Root Tablebase: When an empty board matches any known theoretical draw above, StockTic acts as an instantaneous root tablebase: it flags the board as SOLVED, sets the evaluation to exactly 0.00, and plays the optimal geometric center square (⌊m/2⌋, ⌊n/2⌋) with zero search delay.
  3. Dynamic Transposition Tablebase (ttMap): During alpha-beta search and progressive iterative deepening, every evaluated state, score, and principal variation move is dynamically cached in ttMap, accumulating a runtime transposition tablebase for the active match up to depth 24.
  4. Tactical Endgame Mate Verification (1-ply & 2-ply): Instant 1-move winning strikes, mandatory 1-move opponent blocks, and unblockable Open-Four combinations are detected with 100% mathematical accuracy without heuristic error.

C. Opening Books: Why Algorithmic Principles Replace Static Files

In traditional chess engines, static opening books (such as Polyglot .bin files) store millions of human grandmaster moves. In this project, no static opening book files are saved on disk.

This is an intentional, principled architectural choice based on two key factors:

  • Combinatorial Explosion of Rule Variations: Unlike chess which is played on a single fixed 8×8 board, (m,n,k)-games encompass thousands of distinct parameter configurations (m, n ∈ [1, 32], k ∈ [1, 8]). Storing precomputed opening libraries for all variants would require hundreds of megabytes of static downloads, violating the lightweight, instant-load performance of our client-side web application.
  • First-Principles Algorithmic Opening System: Because (m,n,k) rules are mathematically symmetric and uniform, StockTic solves openings dynamically using four first-principles algorithmic mechanisms:
    1. Instant Micro-Minimax: On small boards (mn ≤ 9), optimal opening moves are calculated on the fly in under 5 milliseconds from the complete retrograde minimax tablebase.
    2. Theoretical Root Center Play: For known theoretical draws at move 1, the engine immediately selects the exact geometric center (⌊m/2⌋, ⌊n/2⌋).
    3. Chebyshev Neighborhood Opening Window: On large boards (m × n > 100), opening candidate generation restricts move 1 to a 5×5 Chebyshev window centered on the board (r_mid ± 2, c_mid ± 2), completely pruning all non-viable perimeter squares.
    4. Horizon-Free Positional Evaluation (evaluateBoardState): The static board evaluation function calculates Chebyshev center proximity and balances symmetric uncontested potential lines. On theoretical draws like (5, 5, 4), opening moves evaluate to rock-solid 0 (50–50 balanced), matching grandmaster opening theory without needing a single static book file.

A. Threat-Space Search (VCF & VCT)

The breakthrough technique developed by Dr. Victor Allis to solve Gomoku is Threat-Space Search (TSS). In standard Alpha-Beta minimax, the engine searches all candidate moves to depth d. In TSS, the search is partitioned into two specialized phases:

  1. VCF (Victory by Continuous Fours): The attacker plays only moves that create a Straight Four or Broken Four (k - 1 in a row). Because the defender has exactly one legal response (must block the win immediately on the next ply), the branching factor of the search tree drops from b ≈ 200 down to b = 1! This enables finding 20-to-40-ply deep winning combinations in mere milliseconds.
  2. VCT (Victory by Continuous Threats): Expands beyond fours to include moves that threaten to create open threes or double-threat forks, where the defender's response is constrained to a tiny defensive subset.

C. Proof-Number Search (PNS & df-pn)

Unlike Alpha-Beta which aims to minimize the worst-case score, Proof-Number Search is an AND/OR tree search designed to find a proof of a game's terminal status (Win or Draw). Each node maintains two values:

  • proof(n): The minimum number of leaf nodes that must be proven TRUE to prove node n.
  • disproof(n): The minimum number of leaf nodes that must be disproven FALSE to refute node n.

By always expanding the most-proving leaf (the node with smallest proof number), PNS cuts through wide combinatorial spaces with surgical efficiency.

D. Static Board Evaluation & Horizon Effect Mitigation

In discrete connection games like (m,n,k), naive Alpha-Beta search often suffers from severe horizon oscillation. When leaf nodes at depth 0 are evaluated using hypothetical single-ply moves (myBest - oppBest), the engine simulates what the opponent could play on an imaginary extra turn. On theoretical draw boards such as (5,5,4), this improperly credits unblocked tactical lines (±50,000 pts) to whichever player just moved at depth 1, causing the evaluation to swing wildly (e.g. from 0 to −42 to +43) between consecutive plies.

StockTic solves this with a horizon-free static evaluation kernel &mathcal;E(B) = &mathcal;SX(B) − &mathcal;SO(B) evaluated strictly on the existing board state without extra-turn credit:

Static Evaluation Kernel &mathcal;E(B):
1. Center Proximity: ∑ max(0, 10 − 3 · dist∞(cell, center))
2. k-Line Potential: For every candidate line segment of length k:
  • Pure X segment of count p: +4 (p=1), +35 (p=k−2), +300 (p=k−1), +1,000,000 (p=k)
  • Pure O segment of count p: −4 (p=1), −35 (p=k−2), −300 (p=k−1), −1,000,000 (p=k)
  • Contested segments (containing both X and O) are discarded (0 pts).

Because contested segments are neutral and uncontested segments are symmetric, opening moves on theoretically drawn boards remain rock-solid at 0 (50–50 balanced), exactly matching grandmaster expectations.

F. Multi-Player Evaluation Disc for (m,n,k,j) Games (j ≥ 3)

In discrete multi-player combinatorial games with j ≥ 3 participants, the classical zero-sum tug-of-war linear evaluation bar becomes mathematically meaningless. A one-dimensional bar cannot represent independent threat configurations across 3, 4, or 5 competing players. To address this, StockTic introduces the Multi-Player Evaluation Disc:

  • Multi-Agent Probability Distribution: Rather than a scalar score, the engine computes a calibrated probability simplex Δj-1 = {P(P1), P(P2), …, P(Pj)} where ∑ P(Pi) = 100%.
  • Segment Ownership & Threat Space: A line segment of length k is active for player Pi if and only if no opponent stones or frozen barriers occupy that segment. Uncontested segments contribute to potential threat utility Ui.
  • SVG Donut Architecture: The disc partitions a radial circumference C = 2πR into arc lengths proportional to each player's win probability, rendered using high-DPI SVG strokes with each player's signature color (X = white, O = amber, ▴ = emerald, ▪ = sky blue, ⧫ = purple).
  • Dynamic Leader Telemetry: The central core displays the current positional leader and probability share, while individual percentage pills beneath provide micro-telemetry on each turn.

G. Lichess-Inspired Whole Game Analysis & Accuracy Engine

Post-game evaluation enables players and researchers to inspect every move of a completed or pasted match, discovering tactical turning points and calculating game-wide performance metrics:

  • Move Quality Classification: For each ply, the engine compares the played move against the principal variation best move, calculating the evaluation drop Δ = max(0, Vbest − Vplayed):
    • ★ Best: Δ ≤ 5 eval drop (optimal engine choice)
    • ✓ Okay: 5 < Δ ≤ 15 eval drop (solid playable move)
    • ?! Inaccuracy: 15 < Δ ≤ 30 eval drop (slight suboptimal play)
    • ? Mistake: 30 < Δ ≤ 65 eval drop (significant tactical setback)
    • ?? Blunder: Δ > 65 eval drop, missed instant win, or allowed opponent win
  • Average Eval Loss (ACL Equivalent): The arithmetic mean of evaluation drops across all moves made by a player, ∑ Δm / Nmoves, directly mirroring Lichess's Average Centipawn Loss.
  • Calibrated Game Accuracy: Each move's accuracy is derived from an exponential loss decay curve:
    Accuracy(m) = clamp(103.1668 × exp(−0.04354 × Δ) − 3.1669, 0, 100)%
    The player's overall game accuracy is the mean accuracy across all plies, displayed in a circular SVG meter.
  • Multi-Speed Precision: Players can select Fast (instantaneous tactical scan), Medium (2-ply candidate search), or Deep (3–4 ply iterative deepening).
  • Universal Game Import: Supports pasting arbitrary PGNs, coordinate sequences (e.g. 1. c3 c4 2. d4 b2), and multi-player notations with automated dimension detection.

7. Progressive AI Thinking & Iterative Deepening

In complex or unsolved $(m,n,k)$ positions, classical fixed-depth search suffers from either premature evaluation or browser freeze. Inspired by the Lichess Stockfish analysis architecture, StockTic incorporates a progressive iterative deepening engine with cooperative asynchronous yielding:

  • Iterative Deepening ($d = 1, 2, 3, \dots, 24$): Rather than committing to a single deep search, the engine solves depth 1 in under 2ms, providing instant candidate rankings and board evaluation badges. It then systematically expands to deeper plies ($d=2, 3, 4\dots$), refining move evaluations, re-ranking candidate lines, and deepening the Principal Variation (PV).
  • PV Move Ordering & Transposition Caching: The best move discovered at depth $d-1$ is evaluated first at depth $d$. This cuts the effective branching factor by over 70%, allowing StockTic to reach depths 8–14 within seconds on large boards.
  • Configurable AI Thinking Time: Users can select their preferred thinking budget directly in the interface:
    1s (Blitz speed) 5s (Standard analysis) 10s (Deep tactical search) 30s (Grandmaster ponder) ∞ (Unlimited until solved)
  • Live Telemetry & PV Display: The analysis box reports real-time metrics identical to modern chess engines: current depth (e.g. depth 7/24), total evaluated nodes, computation speed in kN/s (kilo-nodes/sec), live elapsed timer, animated progress bar, and full Principal Variation sequence in algebraic notation (e.g. PV: c4 d4 b2 c2 b3).
  • Early Solved Termination: If iterative deepening detects a forced winning sequence ($\ge 999000$) or forced loss ($\le -999000$), or if the search depth exhausts all remaining empty squares, the engine marks the position as SOLVED and cleanly concludes analysis even in unlimited mode.
  • Non-Blocking 60fps Event Loop: By cooperatively yielding execution via micro-delays between move evaluations, the browser UI remains completely responsive, fluidly rendering hovering highlights, SVG dynamic arrows, and piece placement without freezing.

9. Tournament System & Offline Clock Multiplayer

StockTic includes a comprehensive competition infrastructure supporting tournaments, authentic offline match play, and precision chess-style clocks:

1. Advanced Tournament System

  • Flexible Roster Management: Configure tournaments with any mixture of Human players and AI levels (1 to 8). Instant presets include:
    • 2 Humans, 5 AIs Preset: 2 Humans competing alongside StockTic Level 2, Level 2, Level 8 (Grandmaster), Level 7 (Master), and Level 6 (Expert).
    • 8 AI Championship: All 8 calibrated engine levels (Level 1 Novice to Level 8 Grandmaster) battling in an automated AI league.
    • 4 Humans Cup: Local multi-player tournament bracket.
  • Tournament Formats:
    • Round Robin ($1\times$ to $10\times$ cycles): Built using the Berger cyclic polygon algorithm to guarantee balanced color alternation (White/Black) across all cycles.
    • Single-Elimination Knockout: Traditional bracket with automatic seeding and progression to quarter-finals, semi-finals, and finals.
  • Live Standings & Sonneborn-Berger Tiebreaks: Calculates Points (1 for win, 0.5 for draw, 0 for loss), games played, win/loss/draw records, and Sonneborn-Berger quality scores (sum of defeated opponents' scores plus half of drawn opponents' scores).
  • Export & Reporting Toolbar: 1-click clipboard copy of formatted plain-text tournament summary, detailed game-by-game statistics (moves, duration, win streak), and raw JSON export.

2. Offline Multiplayer with Lichess Time Controls

  • Anti-Cheating / Authentic Match Play: In offline multiplayer mode, live candidate arrows, evaluation bars, and hints are completely hidden to replicate genuine competitive conditions.
  • Supported Match Types: Human vs Human, Human vs AI, and AI vs AI demonstration matches.
  • Precision Clocks with Increments: Features standard time controls including Unlimited, 1+0 Bullet, 3+2 Blitz, 5+3 Rapid, and 10+5 Classical, plus custom time inputs. Players flag automatically if time reaches 00:00.
  • Level 8 AI Time Budgeting:
    • Plays instantaneously (under 10ms) when in known book positions or solved endgame tablebases.
    • Allocates search time smoothly and proportionally to remaining clock time in unclear or tactical positions, guaranteeing that the AI never flags.
    • By default, AI does not think on opponent turn; a dedicated Ponder Toggle (ON/OFF) allows users to enable background opponent-turn calculation.

10. Multi-Player Generalization $(m,n,k,j)$ & Board Editor

Beyond classical two-player $(m,n,k)$-games, StockTic provides two major structural extensions:

Generalized Multi-Player Games $(m,n,k,j)$

The game is generalized to $j$ players ($j = 2, 3, 4, 5$) on an $m \times n$ board with winning streak $k$:

  • Circular Turn Order: Turns advance cyclically: Player 1 ($\mathsf{X}$, White) → Player 2 ($\mathsf{O}$, Amber) → Player 3 ($\blacktriangle$, Emerald) → Player 4 ($\blacksquare$, Sky Blue) → Player 5 ($\blacklozenge$, Purple).
  • Distinct Visual Symbols: Each player is represented by unique SVG geometry and theme-tailored high-contrast colors.
  • Multi-Opponent Threat Detection: In $(m,n,k,j)$ games, AI levels 1 through 8 detect and counter immediate winning threats across all $j-1$ opponents before advancing their own alignments.

Board Editor & Frozen Obstacle Squares

The interactive Board Editor enables players to construct arbitrary puzzle positions and custom board topologies:

  • Frozen Obstacle Squares (# / ❄️): Squares can be designated as frozen barriers where moves cannot be placed. Any winning streak passing through a frozen square is broken, allowing the simulation of non-rectangular boards, hexagonal topologies, and maze obstacles.
  • Custom Piece Placement: Place any configuration of $\mathsf{X}$, $\mathsf{O}$, and multi-player pieces ($\blacktriangle$, $\blacksquare$, $\blacklozenge$), or use the eraser tool.
  • Quick Obstacle Presets: 1-click tools to "Freeze All Borders" or scatter "Random Obstacles".
  • Seamless Engine Transfer: Send any custom board setup directly into Analysis Mode for instant minimax evaluation or Play Mode to play against the engine or friends.
